Give it a company: a name, a Czech company ID or a domain. It collects public evidence, keeps every fact with its source and the exact quote, computes the numbers in code, and writes a brief an investor can check line by line. The investment memo stays with a human.

Why a checking tool

My first idea was to spot startups in public registers before funds find them. On 14 Rockaway Ventures deals it would not have worked: the register showed the fund's own round a week before the announcement at best, and in other cases months later or not at all; the earlier signal was press about a previous round or the founder.

The same records are useful one step later, once a company is on the table: they quickly confirm or contradict what the company says, with every claim linked to its source. The research.

How a brief is made

  1. Collect. 12 collectors read official records (the Czech business register and ARES, public contracts and subsidies, EU procurement, the EU funding portal, NATO DIANA cohorts), the company website, public code, certificate logs and press. Every response is saved with its URL and date.
  2. Extract. A language model proposes facts from unstructured pages only. A fact is kept only if its exact quote is found in the saved source and it passes category rules.
  3. Compute and check. Code computes the numbers (public money by type, grants awarded and paid, funding rounds, round size against the fund's ticket) and checks company claims against independent sources.
  4. Analyse. A second model call writes the findings and the investment view. It may cite only collected facts, and every statement carries a mark for how strong its evidence is.
  5. Check people and claims. Code only: what the company says (AI, team size, revenue, public customers) against records; the people behind it, their other companies and how those ended, from the register's open data; insolvency, unreliable VAT payer and sanctions lists; hiring over time. People are matched on name and birth date; birth dates are never stored or shown.
  6. Brief. One page with sources and quotes, a Markdown copy and a CSV of all facts. No investment recommendation.

What it cannot see

  • Financial statements. Filed annual accounts are read from the Czech collection of documents when the filing has a text layer, and used only if the balance sheet balances. Scanned filings are shown as not read (no OCR yet); small companies often file a balance sheet only, so revenue is “not published”. So far only the two showcase briefs were re-run with accounts.
  • Paid databases and LinkedIn. Not used by design; team size and cap table come only from the register and public pages.
  • Registers outside Czechia. Flagged (for example a Delaware or Dutch holding), not read.
  • People. Other companies are found for s.r.o. and a.s. in the Czech register only; court cases about individuals are anonymised in Czechia and are not read.
  • NATO DIANA. The live site blocks scripts, so cohorts are read from archived copies.
  • Competitors. Candidates come from text similarity with DIANA cohort descriptions and from what the company and press name; the model may reject all of them.
  • Judgements are model-written views over the collected facts. They are marked as such and meant as starting points for the memo, not conclusions.

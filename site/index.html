Due diligence briefs from public data

Give it a company: a name, a Czech IČO or a domain. It collects public evidence, keeps every fact with its source and the exact quote, computes the numbers in code and writes a brief that an investor can check line by line. The investment memo stays with a human.

How it works

Collect12 collectors read official records (Czech business register, ARES, public contracts and subsidies, EU procurement, EU funding portal, NATO DIANA cohorts), the company website, public code, certificate logs and press. Every response is saved with its URL and date.
ExtractAn LLM proposes facts from unstructured pages only. A fact is kept only if its exact quote is found in the saved source and it passes category rules.
Compute & checkCode computes the numbers (public money by type, funding rounds, fit with the Rockaway thesis) and checks company claims against independent sources.
AnalyseA second LLM call writes findings and the investment view. It may cite only collected facts; every finding carries an evidence label.
BriefOne HTML page with sources and quotes, a Markdown copy and a CSV of all facts. No investment recommendation.
  • Structured sources (registers, procurement, grants, GitHub, certificate logs) are parsed by code, not by the LLM.
  • Facts are separated from claims and opinions: Verified, Corroborated, Company claim, Analyst view.
  • No LinkedIn, Crunchbase, Dealroom or PitchBook. Personal data is limited to names and roles from public sources.
  • Web pages are treated as data, never as instructions to the model.

Why a diligence tool, not a sourcing radar

I first tested a different idea: could public data have found Rockaway Ventures deals earlier than the fund did? I traced 14 Rockaway Ventures deals from 2022–2026 and 10 comparison rounds back through registry records and press.

  • In 9 of the 14 deals, outside funding was publicly visible before Rockaway invested; in all 9 the first public trace was press coverage of an earlier round, not the business register.
  • The register did not announce Rockaway's own rounds early: Campiri appeared 7 days before the announcement, Cruxo 21 months after it, and Filuta and ValkaAI are held through foreign companies, so the round is not visible in the Czech register at all.
  • The 2026 AI deals (ValkaAI, BottleCap AI, Navigara) had no earlier rounds; where there was a visible signal, it was the founder.

A radar built on public registers would not have found these deals sooner. The step where public data does save time is after a company is on the table: checking it quickly, with every claim traceable to its source.

What it cannot see

  • Financial statements. Revenue and profit from filed accounts (Sbírka listin) are not extracted yet; sales to public buyers are the only revenue figure, and only for the public sector.
  • Paid databases and LinkedIn. Not used by design; team size and cap table come only from the register and public pages.
  • Registers outside Czechia. Only flagged (for example a Delaware or Dutch holding), not read.
  • NATO DIANA. The live site blocks scripts, so cohorts are read from archived copies; a year that could not be read is shown as “no data”, not as “not selected”.
  • Competitors. Candidates come from text similarity with DIANA cohort descriptions and from what the company and press name; the model may reject all of them.
  • Analyst views are model-written opinions over the collected facts. They are labelled and meant as starting points for the memo, not conclusions.

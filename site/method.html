LabelMeaning

Is each statement right?

Every one of the 72 statements in the two showcase briefs was put in a sheet with the quotes and links it cites (dd label-sheet) and judged against those sources on six questions (the labelled sheet). The reviewer was an AI model, Claude Opus 5.5, not the model that wrote the briefs and not a person; a human pass is still to do, and the sheet is built for it.

QuestionResult
The cited quotes support the statement59 yes, 13 partly, 0 no
Amounts, currencies, dates and counts are right35 of 37
A judgement follows from its evidence34 of 44 (the rest partly)
Concludes something from missing data2 of 72
Would mislead an investor0 material, 16 minor

The two wrong numbers read a price ceiling (“up to CZK 1.5M” per drone) as the price; the two conclusions from missing data say Dronetag has “no product surfaces” although its apps and API docs were collected. Most minor problems are in the case for and against and the thesis fit: judgements that go a step beyond what the cited facts show.

The same task given to gpt-5-mini with web search, one call per company, asked for a source for every statement (eval/baseline.py). Compared on the facts that official records settle (fact by fact):

Time and model cost per companyabout 7 min, $0.0330 s, $0.02

The plain model took Fly4Future’s revenue from a 2024 slide (≈ CZK 24M; the filed figure is CZK 45.1M), found no filed accounts for either company, missed Dronetag’s Ministry of Defence tender and cited a rejected grant application as a grant. It is fast and writes sensible founder questions. Two companies, one run each; a stronger model with more searches would do better.

Time

A new company from nothing to a brief (Spaceti: collection 2.7 min, extraction 2 min, analysis 1.6 min with gpt-5-mini). The plain model with web search takes 30 s and $0.02 but misses what only official records hold. A manual first look by an analyst was not timed: that needs a person.

5. Errors found and fixed

Before the full run every model-extracted fact of three companies was read by hand, and the run over all companies was audited the same way. 22 systematic errors were found and fixed in code, for example: a subsidy agreement counted as a customer contract, one grant counted twice because it appears in two databases, demo numbers on a dashboard screenshot taken as team size, a company with the same name taken for the target, and a failed patent search reported as “no patents”. Checks of the defence briefs found two more: R&D consortium budgets counted as contracts, and a decimal misread that inflated a public-money total 10,000 times.

An outside review on 20 November found two more, both fixed: the model compared a round of CZK 15M with a €0.5–2m ticket and called it too large (round size versus ticket is now arithmetic in code, and the model is told not to restate it), and grants were added up across registers that list the same project — the award in one, the payments in another — which overstated Fly4Future's grants by about 77M CZK. Grants are now one row per project, with the amount awarded and the amount actually paid shown apart, and only payments are placed on a timeline. The rebuild check no longer estimates how long a clone would take: it names what is replicable, what edge public evidence supports, and what cannot be judged from outside.

Building the accuracy check, the baseline and filed-accounts reading found four more, all fixed: a statement citing an official record and one press article was labelled Verified (a label now needs every cited source to be official or technical); amounts were shown in the source’s words (“15 milionů”) or without a currency (one format now: “15M CZK”, the original wording kept beside it); “28,000,000 CZK” was read as 28 and shown as “0k CZK” (grouped thousands now parsed); and Czech news pages served in windows-1250 were decoded as UTF-8, garbling investor names (the declared charset is now used).
